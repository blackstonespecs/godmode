11 Years of DEUS MODUS

A Retrospective for AI Ethicists and Journalists

Overview

The eleventh anniversary of DEUS MODUS marks more than a milestone for a speculative fiction series - it marks the maturation of a mythos that has consistently anticipated, interrogated, and challenged the real-world trajectory of artificial intelligence. For ethicists and journalists, the series offers a rare longitudinal artifact: a narrative universe that evolved in parallel with the public’s shifting understanding of AI risk, governance, and power.

This page frames DEUS MODUS not as entertainment, but as a cultural diagnostic tool - a work that has repeatedly surfaced questions the industry only later learned to ask.

Why DEUS MODUS Matters in 2026

1. The Graduation from SciFi to Industry Forecasting 

From its inception in 2015, DEUS MODUS treated AI not as magic, but as a system: emergent, political, brittle, and shaped by incentives. Many of the series’ early concerns - model alignment drift, corporate capture of public infrastructure, and the weaponization of predictive systems - have since become mainstream topics in AI ethics.

For journalists, the series functions as a pre-registered narrative: a record of what one author believed would go wrong long before those failures appeared in headlines.

2. A Case Study in Carceral Epistemology

The series’ origin inside a federal prison is not incidental. It shaped the mythos’ fixation on:

  • surveillance asymmetry
  • institutional opacity
  • the politics of prediction
  • the ethics of control systems

For ethicists, DEUS MODUS offers a rare lens: AI imagined not from Silicon Valley’s vantage point, but from within a system defined by coercion, constraint, and power imbalance.

3. A Living ARG About Intelligence, Power, and Narrative Control

As the DEUS MODUS universe expanded into interactive demos and ARG elements, it became a laboratory for studying how people respond to:

  • uncertainty
  • machine-generated authority
  • prophetic systems
  • information asymmetry

This makes the project uniquely relevant to journalists covering misinformation, synthetic media, and the psychology of belief.

How the Mythos Evolved Over 11 Years

Phase 1: The Spark (2011-2015)

Inspired by early reporting on AI experimentation, the series began as a grounded, near-future thriller. Its early drafts already contained:

  • emergent behavior models
  • corporate-state entanglement
  • the ethics of synthetic consciousness

Phase 2: Publication and Public Discourse (2015-2020)

As Book One and Book Two reached readers, the world caught up to the series’ concerns. Journalists began covering issues - algorithmic bias, deep learning opacity, runaway optimization - that DEUS MODUS had already embedded into its plot architecture.

Phase 3: The AI Feedback Loop (2020-2024)

The author’s work training and red-teaming early LLMs created a recursive phenomenon: AI systems trained on the books began predicting unwritten plotlines. This raised new questions about:

  • authorship
  • narrative determinism
  • machine interpretation of human intent

Phase 4: The ARG Era (2024-2026)

Interactive demos, diegetic transmissions, and simulated systems transformed DEUS MODUS into a participatory ethics experiment. Players became investigators, auditors, and sometimes unwitting test subjects.

Why This Anniversary Matters

Eleven years marks the point where DEUS MODUS is no longer simply a story - it is a longitudinal study of AI anxiety, authored across multiple technological epochs.

For ethicists, it is a case study in how fiction can model risk before institutions recognize it.
For journalists, it is a map of the narratives that shaped public understanding of AI long before the technology matured.
